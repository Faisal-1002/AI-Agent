# Tarnwick Distribution Ltd: Warehouse Operations Manual (2019 Edition)

STATUS: RETIRED. This edition was withdrawn when Tarnwick Distribution Ltd closed its Hollin Ridge depot and moved to handheld-based procedures. It is kept for archive reference only and must not be used to run any current warehouse.

Document owner: Bram, Operations Manager, Hollin Ridge depot
Edition: 2019, revision 3

## 1. Scope and purpose

This manual set out the standard working practices for the Hollin Ridge depot of Tarnwick Distribution Ltd, a regional distributor of general merchandise and chilled food. It covered receiving, put-away, cold chain handling, forklift operation, picking, dispatch and returns. Agency staff were expected to read sections 2 to 4 before their first shift, and team leaders signed a sheet confirming that each new starter had been walked through the cold chain rules in person.

Where this manual conflicted with a customer contract, the contract took priority, and the shift supervisor was responsible for briefing the team on the difference.

## 2. Receiving

All inbound vehicles had to be booked into a dock slot at least 24 hours in advance through the Tarnwick yard planner. Drivers arriving more than 45 minutes after their booked slot were moved to the back of the queue unless the shift supervisor agreed otherwise. Unbooked vehicles were turned away during peak weeks.

Each delivery was checked against the advance shipping notice before unloading began. The receiving clerk counted pallets, inspected stretch wrap and corner boards, and recorded any damage on the paper goods-in sheet. A pallet with more than 5 percent of its cartons crushed was refused at the dock rather than accepted under protest. Under the 2019 rules, every trailer had to be fully unloaded within 75 minutes of the doors opening, and supervisors logged any trailer that overran so that repeat offenders could be discussed with the haulier.

Accepted pallets received a Tarnwick licence plate label with the prefix TWD-HR and were put away within the same shift. Pallets taller than 1.8 metres were broken down before put-away because the upper rack levels at Hollin Ridge had limited clearance.

## 3. Cold chain

At Tarnwick, chilled goods were defined as products to be held between 0 and 5 degrees Celsius, and frozen goods had to be held at or below minus 22 degrees Celsius. The receiving clerk probed one carton from every fourth pallet of chilled stock using a calibrated probe thermometer, and two cartons from every frozen pallet.

A chilled load that arrived above 5 degrees but no warmer than 8 degrees could be accepted only with the duty manager's approval, and it had to be moved into the chiller within 15 minutes of being unloaded. Any chilled product that spent more than 20 minutes outside its range inside the building was placed in the quarantine cage and assessed by the quality lead before it could be released or written off. Frozen product that showed signs of thawing, such as soft cartons or ice crystals on the outer packaging, was always rejected.

Chiller and freezer doors were fitted with alarms that sounded after 3 minutes open. Temperature records were downloaded from the room loggers every morning by the first-shift team leader and kept for 18 months. Probe thermometers were checked against a reference unit every week and replaced if they read more than half a degree out.

## 4. Forklifts and powered equipment

Only staff holding a current Tarnwick internal licence could operate counterbalance trucks, reach trucks or powered pallet trucks. Licences were renewed every 36 months after a refresher course and practical test. Agency drivers had to show an equivalent licence from their agency and complete a two-hour site familiarisation before driving on their own.

Speed limits under the 2019 manual were 10 km/h on the open yard, 6 km/h inside racking aisles and walking pace in the loading bays. Operators sounded the horn at every aisle end and at blind corners, and gave way to pedestrians at all marked crossings.

Pre-use checks covered brakes, horn, lights, mast chains, forks and battery or gas level, and had to be recorded on the paper checklist hung at the charging bay. A truck that failed any check was tagged out with a red card and reported to the maintenance contractor, and it could not be used again until the contractor signed the card off. Forks had to be lowered to floor level whenever a truck was parked, keys were returned to the key cabinet at the end of each shift, and passengers were never allowed.

## 5. Picking and dispatch

Pick lists were printed in waves, with three waves per shift. The depot's picking accuracy target was 99.5 percent, measured by a random check of 30 orders per shift. Orders confirmed before 16:30 were dispatched the same evening; anything later went on the next morning's first trailer.

Loaded trailers were sealed in front of the driver, and the seal reference was written on the dispatch note. Mixed chilled and ambient loads were only allowed in trailers with a fitted bulkhead.

## 6. Returns

Customer returns were accepted within 28 days of delivery and inspected within two working days of arrival. Inspected items were graded A (resaleable), B (repack needed) or C (dispose), and the grade was recorded on the returns log. Chilled returns were never resold.

## 7. Housekeeping and safety

Pedestrian walkways were painted green and kept clear at all times. Spills were reported to the shift supervisor immediately and the area was coned off until cleaned. Fire doors and extinguisher points were checked weekly. Near misses were recorded on the shift report before the end of the same shift.

## 8. Why this edition was retired

Tarnwick Distribution replaced the paper checklists and goods-in sheets described here with handheld devices, and the Hollin Ridge depot itself closed after its lease ended. The thresholds in this document reflect Tarnwick's 2019 practices only. Staff of any other organisation should follow their own current warehouse manual, which may set different temperatures, speed limits, unloading targets and licence periods.
