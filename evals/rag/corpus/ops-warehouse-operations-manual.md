# Qamar Freight - Warehouse Operations Manual

Document code: WOM-THR-04. Version 4.2. Applies to the Thamra Inland Hub.
Document owner: Rania, Head of Warehouse Operations.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This manual sets out how Qamar Freight receives, stores, picks and dispatches goods at the Thamra Inland Hub. It applies to every warehouse associate, shift supervisor, agency worker, contractor and visiting driver who works inside the hub perimeter. Where a customer contract sets a stricter rule than this manual, the contract rule applies and the shift supervisor must record the exception in the customer instruction file kept at the supervisors' desk.

The hub operates around the clock on three shifts and handles ambient, chilled and frozen freight for retail, grocery and light-industrial customers. Most inbound volume arrives from Port Kheraz on the coastal corridor, and most outbound volume leaves on the regional store-delivery network. Because the same dock doors serve both inbound and outbound traffic, the rules on timing in this manual matter as much as the rules on handling.

New starters must read sections 2 to 8 before their first shift and sign the training record to confirm they have done so. Supervisors must also read sections 9 and 10.

## 2. Receiving

### 2.1 Dock appointments

All inbound trailers must hold a booked dock appointment. Appointments are issued in 30-minute slots between 05:00 and 23:30, and the gatehouse checks the booking reference before a trailer is allowed past the barrier. A carrier that arrives more than 30 minutes after its slot loses the slot and is rebooked into the next free appointment, which may fall on the following shift. The gatehouse records a No-Show Flag against any carrier that does not arrive at all, and repeated flags are reviewed by the Commercial team at the end of each quarter.

Trailers waiting in the yard must park in the marked holding lanes with the engine off. Drivers stay in the driver lounge beside the gatehouse and may only enter the dock area when called forward and wearing a high-visibility vest.

### 2.2 Unloading and inspection

Once a trailer is docked, the receiving team has 90 minutes to unload, inspect and confirm the delivery. The clock starts when the trailer is chocked at the dock door, not when it passes the gate. For chilled and frozen trailers, the receiving clerk must read the trailer's temperature record before the first pallet leaves the trailer. If the record shows a reading outside the agreed range, the load is received on hold and moved straight to the quarantine zone.

Each pallet is checked for label match, carton count, visible damage and wrap integrity. A carton count difference of up to two cartons per pallet is corrected on the Goods Received Note by the receiving clerk. Larger differences require the shift supervisor to sign the Goods Received Note and notify the customer service desk before the trailer is released. Photographs of any damage are attached to the receiving record in the warehouse management system.

### 2.3 Labelling and put-away

Every accepted pallet receives a Pallet ID label at the receiving bay. The label is a ten-character code beginning with the letter P, and it must be applied to two adjacent faces of the pallet so that it can be scanned from either aisle. Put-away tasks are generated automatically by the warehouse management system and must be completed within four hours of Goods Received Note confirmation.

Cross-dock freight is not put away. It is staged in the cross-dock lane at Doors 1 to 3 and must leave the building within six hours of arrival. Cross-dock pallets that miss their outbound trailer are put away in ZONE-C7 and reported to the Duty Manager.

## 3. Storage zones

The hub is divided into storage zones, each with a code painted on the floor at the zone entrance and repeated on every rack end. Associates must only place stock in a zone that matches the storage class shown on the put-away task. If the system directs a pallet to a zone that looks wrong, the associate stops and asks the shift supervisor rather than choosing another location.

ZONE-A1 holds fast-moving ambient goods on ground-level pick faces close to the dispatch doors. Replenishment for ZONE-A1 is triggered when a pick face falls below 25 percent of its capacity, and replenishment tasks take priority over put-away tasks during a pick wave.

ZONE-A2 holds slower-moving ambient goods in standard selective racking with up to five beam levels. Aisles in ZONE-A2 are kept at a minimum clear width of 3.1 metres for reach truck operation.

ZONE-B4 is the secure cage for high-value goods such as consumer electronics, small kitchen appliances and boxed cosmetics. Access to ZONE-B4 is restricted to associates whose badge carries the B4 permission, and every entry is logged by the cage door reader. ZONE-B4 uses high-bay racking up to 11 metres, so only reach truck operators who hold the High-Bay Endorsement may put away or retrieve pallets there.

ZONE-C2 is the bulk floor-stacking area for heavy, robust goods such as bottled water and canned food. Pallets in ZONE-C2 may be stacked a maximum of three high, and only when the bottom pallet carries no more than 1,000 kg.

ZONE-C7 is the ambient overflow zone. It is opened only when ZONE-A2 is more than 92 percent full, and any stock placed there must be moved back to its home zone within ten days.

ZONE-F1 is the chilled store and ZONE-F3 is the frozen store. Their temperature limits and door rules are described in section 4.

ZONE-Q1 is the quarantine zone. It holds any stock that is damaged, mislabelled, recalled or suspected of a temperature excursion. Nothing may be picked from ZONE-Q1. A Quarantine Disposition Request is raised for each pallet, and the Quality Lead must make a disposition decision within 48 hours of the pallet entering the zone. The decision is one of release, rework, return to supplier or write-off.

ZONE-R5 is the customer returns area. Returned goods are sorted within two working days and are either restocked, sent to ZONE-Q1 or consolidated for return to the supplier.

## 4. Cold chain

### 4.1 Temperature thresholds

The chilled store ZONE-F1 must stay between 2 °C and 8 °C. A high-temperature alarm is raised when the air temperature stays above 8.5 °C for more than 12 consecutive minutes, and a low-temperature alarm is raised when it stays below 1.5 °C for the same period. The frozen store ZONE-F3 has a target of -20 °C and an acceptable band from -23 °C to -18 °C. The frozen alarm is raised as soon as the temperature rises above -15 °C, regardless of how long the rise lasts.

Temperature probes in both zones record a reading every five minutes, and the readings are kept for two years. Probes are calibrated every six months by the approved calibration contractor, and a probe that fails calibration must be replaced on the same day. Alarms sound at the supervisors' desk and on the handheld terminals of every associate badged for the cold stores.

### 4.2 Doors and loading

Cold store doors must not be held open for more than 45 seconds during normal put-away and picking. Chilled outbound trailers must be pre-cooled to 4 °C, and frozen trailers to -18 °C, before the first pallet is loaded, and the loader records the pre-cool reading on the dispatch checklist. A chilled trailer may stay open at the dock for a maximum of 20 minutes during loading or unloading. If the work will take longer, the doors are closed and the refrigeration unit is allowed to recover before the work continues.

### 4.3 Excursions

A temperature excursion is any period where product or air temperature is outside the zone band for long enough to raise an alarm. When an excursion lasts more than 30 minutes, every pallet in the affected area is placed on hold and moved to ZONE-Q1, and the shift supervisor informs the Quality Lead within one hour. Product is never relabelled or re-dated to hide an excursion, and the alarm record is attached to each Quarantine Disposition Request.

## 5. Picking and dispatch

Orders are released to the floor in waves. An order is Ready To Wave only when every line has stock allocated in a pickable zone. Chilled and frozen goods are picked first-expired, first-out, while ambient goods are picked first-in, first-out unless the customer asks otherwise. The dawn wave closes at 10:30 and the dusk wave at 18:30, and a wave close report goes to the Duty Manager within 30 minutes of each wave closing.

Every outbound pallet is stretch-wrapped with at least five turns at the base and three at the top, and is labelled with its delivery route code. Dispatch loads are checked against the load plan by a second associate before the trailer doors are sealed. The seal number is written on the dispatch checklist and repeated on the driver's delivery papers.

## 6. Shift handover

The hub runs three shifts: the Dawn shift from 06:00 to 14:00, the Dusk shift from 14:00 to 22:00, and the Night shift from 22:00 to 06:00. Each shift overlaps the next by a 15-minute handover window so that supervisors can walk the floor together.

Handover takes place at the supervisors' desk beside Door 9 and is recorded on the handover log, form HO-3. The outgoing shift supervisor completes HO-3 and must sign it before leaving the site, and the incoming supervisor countersigns to confirm they have read it. HO-3 must list open receiving exceptions, cold chain alarms raised during the shift, equipment that is out of service or behaving unusually, agency staff on site, and customer escalations that are still open.

If the handover has not been completed and countersigned within 20 minutes of the shift change, the incoming supervisor escalates to the Duty Manager. Supervisors may not hand over by message alone. A face-to-face handover is required, with HO-3 open on the desk, and any item that is not written on HO-3 is treated as not handed over.

## 7. Forklift and equipment certification

### 7.1 Equipment classes

Material handling equipment at the hub falls into three certification classes. Class C covers counterbalance forklifts used in the yard, at the docks and in ZONE-C2. Class R covers reach trucks used in racked aisles. Class P covers powered pallet trucks used on the dock floor and in the cold stores. An operator may only drive the classes shown on their certification card, which must be carried at all times while driving.

### 7.2 Initial certification

New operators complete a three-day course of 24 classroom and practical hours for each class they will drive, followed by a practical assessment with a certified assessor. The pass mark for the practical assessment is 85 percent. Any critical fault, such as travelling with raised forks or driving without a seat belt, is an automatic fail regardless of the score. Operators who fail may retake the assessment once, after ten working days of supervised practice.

### 7.3 Validity and recertification

Certification is valid for 18 months from the assessment date. Operators must pass a refresher assessment before their certification expires, and the Training Coordinator sends a reminder eight weeks before the expiry date. An operator whose certification has lapsed may not drive any equipment until they pass the refresher.

Reach truck operators who work in ZONE-B4, or in any racking above eight metres, also need the High-Bay Endorsement. The endorsement requires a further day of training at height and a separate assessment, and it is renewed together with the Class R certification.

### 7.4 Daily checks and incidents

Before the first use of any truck on a shift, the operator completes the pre-use checklist MHE-7, covering forks, chains, hydraulics, tyres, horn, lights, seat belt and battery or gas level. A truck that fails any item on MHE-7 is tagged out of service, parked in the equipment bay and recorded on HO-3.

Any operator involved in a collision, a dropped load or a near miss involving equipment is stood down from driving for the rest of the shift. They may return to driving only after the shift supervisor has reviewed the event and, for collisions, after a reassessment by a certified assessor.

## 8. Traffic and housekeeping

Equipment speed is limited to 8 km/h inside the building and to 5 km/h in marked pedestrian zones, at dock doors and inside the cold stores. Pedestrian walkways are marked in green and must be kept clear at all times. Associates on foot must make eye contact with an equipment operator before crossing in front of a truck.

Broken pallets, loose wrap and used strapping are removed to the waste bay at the end of every wave. Spills inside the cold stores must be cleaned within 15 minutes because they freeze quickly and create slip hazards. The Dawn shift supervisor walks every zone once a week with a housekeeping checklist and records the score on the notice board.

## 9. Inventory accuracy

Cycle counts follow a fixed calendar. ZONE-B4 is counted every week, ZONE-F1 and ZONE-F3 every two weeks, and all other storage zones once a month. A location passes the count if the counted quantity is within 0.5 percent of the system quantity. Any larger variance triggers a blind recount by a different associate within 24 hours, and variances confirmed on recount that are worth more than SAR 5,000 are reported to the Head of Warehouse Operations on the same day.

The hub-wide inventory accuracy target is 99.6 percent of locations correct at count. Results are published to all shifts every Monday, together with the three locations that caused the largest variances in the previous week.

## 10. Document control

This manual is reviewed every twelve months, or sooner after any serious incident. Proposed changes are sent to the Head of Warehouse Operations, who approves each new version. Version 4.2 replaces version 4.1. It adds the 20-minute limit for open chilled trailers, the equipment section of HO-3 and the High-Bay Endorsement rules. Printed copies are uncontrolled; the current version is always the one held in the document library.
