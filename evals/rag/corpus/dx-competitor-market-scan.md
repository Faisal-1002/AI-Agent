# Regional Freight Market Scan: Velmora Coast and Tessaline Plains

Prepared by Halverstone Freight Insights
Lead analyst: Mirela, Senior Analyst, Ground Transport Practice
Edition: autumn scan, internal circulation only

## Purpose of this scan

This scan summarises how four independent freight operators active across the Velmora Coast and the Tessaline Plains price and package their services. It is meant as a quick orientation for planning teams, not as a procurement recommendation. All prices are list prices quoted in Velmoran dollars (VD) before negotiated discounts, fuel surcharges and seasonal levies. Each operator uses its own zone scheme, so zone names in this document are not interchangeable between companies, and a "Zone C" at one operator has nothing to do with a "Zone C" anywhere else.

## Method

Mirela's team collected public rate cards, interviewed eleven shippers who use at least two of the operators, and ran three test bookings per operator for a standard pallet (1.2 m by 1.0 m, up to 600 kg) and a standard parcel (up to 15 kg). Transit times below are the median of the observed test bookings rather than the advertised promise. Where an operator would not quote a lane, the gap is noted rather than estimated.

## Kestrelvane Logistics

Kestrelvane is the largest operator in the scan, holding an estimated 23 percent of contract pallet volume on the Velmora Coast. It divides its network into five rings, labelled KV-R1 (the port cluster) through KV-R5 (the far inland depots). Its flagship pallet product, sold under the code KVL-PAL-STD, is priced at VD 58 per pallet within KV-R1 and rises by roughly VD 9 per additional ring. Next-morning delivery is sold as an add-on, KVL-AM, for a flat VD 14 per consignment.

Shippers praised Kestrelvane's tracking portal but complained that ring boundaries move each spring, which makes annual budgeting awkward. The median observed transit for a KV-R3 pallet was 31 hours.

## Bluefen Cargo Lines

Bluefen is smaller but growing quickly through chilled and frozen freight. Its zones are named by compass point and band, for example Bluefen Zone N2 or Bluefen Zone S1. The refrigerated overnight service is sold under product code BFC-CHILL-9, which guarantees a trailer set-point between 2 and 7 degrees Celsius and costs VD 112 per pallet in any band-one zone. A frozen variant, BFC-FRZ-18, holds minus 18 degrees and adds a VD 26 premium on top of the chilled rate.

Bluefen's ambient pallet rate is not competitive, at VD 71 in band one, and the company openly positions ambient freight as a way to fill spare space on its chilled lanes. Shippers rated its temperature logging highly and its customer service as patchy outside office hours.

## Corvatta Freight

Corvatta is the price leader. It uses a letter scheme, Corvatta Zone A through Corvatta Zone E, where Zone A is the metro core around the Velmora port and Zone E covers the upland routes of the Tessaline Plains. The standard pallet product, CVF-PS-12 (marketed as "Pallet Saver"), costs VD 44 per pallet in Zone A and VD 52.50 per pallet in Zone C. Corvatta achieves these prices by consolidating loads twice a day, which means pallets can wait up to a day at the hub before departure.

The median observed transit for a Corvatta Zone C pallet was 46 hours, the slowest in the scan. Several shippers described Corvatta as the right choice for replenishment stock that is not time-critical, and the wrong choice for anything promotional.

## Nimbro Parcel

Nimbro does not carry pallets at all and focuses on parcels up to 30 kg. Its zones are numbered 10, 20, 30 and 40 by distance band from its central sortation hall. The standard parcel product, NMB-P15, costs VD 6.80 in band 10 and VD 9.40 in band 40. Nimbro's differentiator is a two-hour delivery window sent to recipients the evening before, and retail shippers told Mirela that this alone justified the price.

## Observations and caveats

The scan found no operator that is both cheapest and fastest. Kestrelvane trades a mid-range price for network reach, Bluefen charges a premium for verified cold chain, Corvatta wins on price at the cost of speed, and Nimbro serves a different segment entirely. Surcharges were excluded from all figures, and fuel levies alone moved effective prices by between 6 and 11 percent during the observation window.

None of these operators publish service-level credits on their standard rate cards; credits are negotiated contract by contract. Mirela recommends re-running the test bookings every six months, because two of the four operators changed their zone maps during the previous year and at least one more is expected to do so before the next scan.
