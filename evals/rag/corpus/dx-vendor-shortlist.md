# Handheld Scanner Vendor Shortlist

Prepared for: the warehouse modernisation working group, Marrowby Freight Co.
Author: Tomasz, Procurement Lead
Status: recommendation for steering review

## Background

Marrowby Freight Co. plans to replace roughly 240 ageing handheld barcode scanners across its three depots. The current devices are out of support, their batteries rarely last a full shift, and the scanning app on them can no longer be updated. Seven vendors responded to the request for proposals; four met the mandatory requirements and were evaluated in detail.

## Evaluation criteria

Each vendor was scored against five weighted criteria: durability (25 percent), software integration with the warehouse management system (25 percent), battery life (20 percent), total cost of ownership over five years (20 percent) and support quality (10 percent). Scores were combined into a weighted total out of 100. Every shortlisted device was tested for two weeks on the receiving and dispatch floors of the largest depot.

## Results

| Rank | Vendor | Device tested | Weighted score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quorrel Devices | QD-Trek 7 | 86 |
| 2 | Sapphene Instruments | Sapphene S40 | 81 |
| 3 | Tindalridge Hardware | TR Handy Pro | 74 |
| 4 | Olvarra Tech | Olvarra Scout | 69 |

Quorrel Devices finished first with a weighted score of 86 out of 100, driven by its QD-Trek 7 handheld, which survived repeated drops onto concrete and ran for 14 hours on a single charge during the trial. Its list price of 640 dollars per unit is the highest of the four, but the five-year cost is competitive because its batteries are rated for twice as many charge cycles.

Sapphene Instruments came second with 81. The Sapphene S40 was the cheapest device at 520 dollars per unit and integrated cleanly, but its battery lasted only about 10 hours, which means a mid-shift swap on long days.

Tindalridge Hardware scored 74. Its device was robust, but integration with the warehouse management system needed a custom connector that the vendor could not deliver within the project timeline.

Olvarra Tech scored 69. The Olvarra Scout performed well on scanning speed, but the company is young, offers support only during its own office hours and could not provide references from customers of a similar size.

## Risks and open points

Two risks remain open. First, Quorrel Devices quoted a lead time of ten weeks for the full order, which would push the last depot past the busy season unless deliveries are staggered. Second, none of the four vendors has yet confirmed how long security updates will be provided for the tested devices, and the working group wants a written commitment before signature.

## Recommendation

The working group recommends entering final negotiation with Quorrel Devices, with Sapphene Instruments held as the fallback if commercial terms cannot be agreed. Before any full rollout, a pilot of 20 units should run at one depot for a month. The final contract value and delivery schedule will be presented at a later steering review once negotiation concludes.
