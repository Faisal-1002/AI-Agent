# Brindlecoast Software Group: IT Security Guidelines

Version 4.2
Owner: Security Desk
Approved by: Renata, Head of Information Security

## 1. Who these guidelines apply to

These guidelines apply to everyone who uses Brindlecoast systems: permanent staff, contractors, interns and partners who have been issued a company account. They describe the minimum expectations; individual teams may set stricter rules for the systems they run, but never weaker ones. If something here is unclear, ask the Security Desk through the service portal or the security channel in company chat before acting.

## 2. Passwords and sign-in

Every staff account must use a password or passphrase of at least 14 characters. Administrator and service-management accounts need at least 20 characters. We strongly recommend passphrases made of several unrelated words, generated and stored in the company password manager, which every employee receives on their first day.

Passwords are not rotated on a fixed schedule. You only need to change a password when there is reason to think it has been exposed, when the Security Desk asks you to, or when you leave a shared role. Never reuse a Brindlecoast password on any other site, and never share it with a colleague, including your manager.

Multi-factor authentication is required on every account that supports it. Use the approved authenticator app or a hardware security key; text-message codes are not accepted as a second factor. After ten failed sign-in attempts an account is locked for 30 minutes, and repeated lockouts are reviewed by the Security Desk.

## 3. Company devices

Company laptops are supplied with full-disk encryption, endpoint protection and automatic updates already enabled. Do not disable any of these. Screens must lock automatically after 5 minutes of inactivity, and you should lock your screen manually whenever you step away. Operating system updates must be installed within 7 days of release, and updates marked critical within 48 hours; laptops that fall behind are restricted to the update service until they catch up.

USB storage devices are blocked on company laptops. To move files, use the approved file-sharing service. Never leave a company device unattended in a vehicle, and carry laptops as hand luggage when travelling.

## 4. Personal devices

Personal phones and tablets may be used for company chat, calendar and mail only through the managed work profile installed by IT. The work profile can be wiped remotely without touching personal data. Source code, customer data and design files must never be stored on a personal device, and personal laptops may not connect to internal systems at all.

## 5. Working away from the office

Public and hotel Wi-Fi networks may only be used with the always-on VPN connected. When working on trains, in cafés or in shared spaces, use the privacy filter supplied with your laptop and avoid taking calls about customer matters where you can be overheard. Printing at home is discouraged; if you must print confidential material, shred it afterwards.

## 6. Handling data

Brindlecoast classifies information into four levels: Public, Internal, Confidential and Restricted. Restricted data, such as customer production data and signing keys, may only be stored in the systems approved for it, and it must never be copied into test or staging environments. When in doubt, treat information as Confidential.

## 7. Reporting security incidents

A security incident is anything that might put Brindlecoast systems or data at risk: a suspicious link you clicked, an unexpected sign-in prompt, a malware warning, a file shared with the wrong person, or a colleague asking you to bypass a control. Report any suspected security incident to the Security Desk within 4 hours of noticing it, even if you are not sure it is real. A false alarm costs us nothing; a late report can cost a great deal.

Lost or stolen devices are treated as urgent and must be reported within 1 hour, so the device can be locked and wiped. Suspicious messages should be reported with the Report Phish button in the mail client rather than forwarded. Do not try to investigate an incident yourself, and do not delete evidence such as messages or files.

The Security Desk acknowledges reports within 30 minutes during business hours and assigns a severity level from SEV-1 (critical, active compromise) to SEV-4 (low, informational). You will be told what happens next and whether you need to do anything.

## 8. Training and exceptions

All new joiners must complete security awareness training within 30 days of starting, and everyone repeats a shorter refresher each year. Exceptions to these guidelines require written approval from the Head of Information Security, are recorded in the exceptions register and expire after six months unless renewed.
